About Us

Who We Are

Far East Snowsports (FES) was established in 2015 in Niseko, Hokkaido. We are a boutique snowsports school built around individually curated guest experiences and the belief that snowsports instruction should be a real, sustainable profession.

As AI continues to reshape the global workforce, human interaction — the kind that happens on a mountain, between a skilled instructor and a curious guest — will only become more valued. Snowsports lessons are not something that can be automated. FES is committed to building the structure, systems, and culture that allow career instructors to lead that future with credibility and confidence.

We think in decades, not seasons. Our ambition is to help shape what the snowsports industry looks like over the next 100 years — and that starts with treating instruction as a long-term career, not a one-winter detour. Everything we've built — OBP, the Support Programme, the Loyalty Programme, the path from Junior to Full Member — is designed so that the value of being part of FES compounds the longer you stay. A first season at FES is valuable. A second, third, fifth, and tenth season are exponentially more so.

FES is also the team behind Stivot (a snowsports school management software) and the Adventure Map — both built from our own operational experience in Niseko.

The Role

The Junior Member Role

Every new member at FES starts as a Junior Member. This is an outsourcing contract — you are an independent contractor, not an employee.

What that means in practice

  • You process your own taxes
  • You organise your own phone
  • FES manages your calendar — lessons are allocated to you, and you request dates to block in advance
  • You take initiative to communicate directly with guests who return to you or are referred to you
  • Returning guests and referrals invoice through you (OBP — see Pay Structure)
  • No membership fee — unlike Full Members, Junior Members have no upfront financial commitment
  • No snow school line-up or standby duties

Contract period: Late November to mid-April

Not a fixed role

Being a Junior Member isn't a junior position in a hierarchy. Within our team, every member is a mentor in some areas and a mentee in others — and that holds whether you're in your first season or your tenth. The Junior Member range itself is wide: some members are early in their career and lean more on being mentored, while others arrive with seasons behind them and end up mentoring more than they're mentored.

What matters most — especially for Junior Members — is the mindset that holds both at once: confident enough to contribute, open enough to keep learning. Once that mindset shows up consistently, alongside the operational efficiency we expect from a Full Member, you'll be invited to step up. For some, that comes in their second season; for others, it takes many years. Where you start shapes the length of the path, not the value of being here.

Workload Reality

What to Expect: First Season Earnings

One of the most common questions from candidates: how many days will I actually work? The honest answer is that it varies significantly — and it is partly in your control.

Assigned days depend on bookings received, weather, and team scheduling. FES does not guarantee a minimum number of working days per season. A typical first-season Junior Member works between 40–70 assigned days (in full-day equivalents), though reality is more nuanced. Roughly half of all lessons are half-days — so the actual number of times you go out to teach is closer to 60–90 individual sessions across the season.

Peak periods

  • Christmas to New Year — the most consistent peak of the season
  • Chinese New Year — late January to mid-February depending on the year; a major driver for FES
  • Australian school holidays — overlaps with late January and February
  • Mid-March — sometimes a second, unexpected surge
  • Easter & SE Asian school holidays — Indonesian, Thai, and Filipino school breaks fall unpredictably through January–March; can quietly extend a slow period into a busy one. Treat as a wildcard.

Quieter periods

  • Early December — snow-dependent, season ramps slowly
  • Early January dip — between New Year and Chinese New Year, bookings can drop sharply for 1–2 weeks
  • Post-CNY dip — once Chinese New Year ends, there is often a brief pause before Australian holidays continue the momentum
  • Late March — season winds down; snowpack and bookings both tail off

Understanding Chinese New Year

Chinese New Year (Spring Festival) is a lunar holiday — the date shifts each year, typically falling between late January and mid-February. For FES, it is one of the most significant demand drivers of the season. The two weeks surrounding CNY generate a sustained surge in bookings from mainland Chinese, Hong Kong, Singaporean, and broader Asian guests.

The pattern: there is usually a dip in early January after the New Year holiday noise clears and before CNY demand builds. Then a sharp peak as CNY week arrives. Then another dip immediately after — often the quietest week of the mid-season. Australian school holiday bookings then bridge into the second half of February.

On unpredictability:

Season-to-season income fluctuates significantly and doesn't follow a clean curve. March can be quiet one year and unexpectedly busy the next. Snowfall timing affects December entirely. CNY demand can shift two to three weeks depending on the lunar calendar. Plan conservatively and treat any upside as a bonus, not a baseline.

OBP supplements assigned work. If you have returning guests or generate your own bookings, OBP sits on top of your assigned days and is available from day one. Members with strong OBP networks can meaningfully increase both their total working sessions and their effective rate.

Financial planning: Don't arrive in Niseko without a buffer. The first few weeks of any season are unpredictable — snow-dependent, weather-affected, and typically light on assigned days. Plan for 3–4 weeks of living costs before reliable income begins.

Pay periods: Bookings close at the end of each calendar month. Payment is made on the 15th of the following month — so your first payout (for December lessons) arrives on 15 January. Note that a booking's end date determines which pay period it belongs to: a lesson that starts in December but ends in January is counted as January billing and paid on 15 February. Factor this into your buffer planning.

Growth

Career Progression: Junior → Full Member

There is no fixed timeline and no fixed criteria checklist. Almost all new members start as a Junior Member — not as a trial, but because building a track record within FES is how we maintain our standard. In rare cases, an instructor with an exceptional professional background — such as a national demo team member or a widely recognised high-profile professional — may be considered for Full Member from the outset. This is the exception, not the rule. Some members transition to Full Member the season after their first. Some take five years. It genuinely depends on the individual.

The size of your client base is one factor, but it is not the only one. We also look at:

  • Your ability to mentor and support other team members
  • How you collaborate within the team
  • Your operational and admin performance — SOP adherence, reporting, communication

Junior Members who reach 50 full working days and return the following season become eligible for equipment support (see Support Programme).

Guiding

Guiding

FES offers guiding services — accompanying guests outside the resort gates into Niseko's wider backcountry terrain. Guiding is not a standard part of every member's season. It is a pathway for members who build the experience, qualifications, and internal track record to earn it.

First-year members in Niseko are not assigned outside-the-gates (OTG) guiding, regardless of qualifications held. The only exception is members who hold IFMGA certification with significant professional ski guiding experience in other areas.

The Path to OTG Guiding

  • Returning member with prior Niseko gate experience
  • Valid avalanche or guiding qualification (AST 1/2, NZMGA Assistant Guide, or equivalent)
  • Attend 3+ FES guiding workshops in the current or prior season
  • Submit a minimum of 2 trip logs using the FES format — at least one must include a 500m+ ascent, at least one must be with another FES member or mentor
  • Pass the FES Internal Guiding Assessment, conducted in-field by designated FES evaluators

The assessment covers decision-making and risk management, Niseko-specific terrain knowledge, guest handling, and safety protocol. It is rigorous by design — we take this seriously because the environment is serious.

If guiding is part of why you're interested in FES, raise it in your interview. It helps us assess fit and start tracking your progression from the beginning.

Life in Niseko

Living in Niseko

Niseko is a ski region built around winter tourism. The infrastructure is good for its size, but the area is a collection of small mountain towns — not a city.

Where FES members live: FES members are spread across Kutchan, Niseko Town, and the Rankoshi/Yunosato area. The majority of FES staff housing is located in Kutchan. Hirafu is where most guests stay and where the in-season nightlife sits, but very few FES members live there.

Hirafu — the tourist hub

  • Most touristy and lively part of the area during the season
  • Concentrated in Upper and Lower Hirafu area
  • Where most off-mountain social activity happens during peak weeks
  • Common destination for guests; less common as a residence for FES staff

Kutchan

  • Nearest real town — ~15 min by car from Hirafu base
  • Where most FES staff housing is located
  • Three major supermarkets, home centres, hardware stores
  • JR train station: Sapporo (~1.5 hrs), Otaru (~1 hr)

Cost of Living

  • Hokkaido is generally more expensive than the Japanese average for groceries and daily essentials
  • Kutchan's three major supermarkets keep day-to-day living manageable on a normal budget
  • Compared to Canada, the US, New Zealand, or much of Europe, Japan still works out noticeably cheaper across food, transport, and entertainment
  • Hirafu restaurant and bar pricing during peak season is an exception — built around international tourism
  • FES members get discounts at Pow Bar (organic café) and Pyram (organic food shop)
  • If you try to cook and shop as you might at home, costs can run noticeably higher. Eating and living the way most Japanese people do — local supermarkets, seasonal produce, simple home cooking — keeps daily costs low and is genuinely worth exploring. Japan is widely associated with one of the world's longest-lived, healthiest populations, and the everyday food culture that underpins that is affordable, accessible, and often better than what you'd find at home.

Community & Environment

  • The international instructor community in Niseko is small and tight-knit across schools — you will encounter the same people throughout the season
  • Japanese language classes: Smile language school operates locally; FES can organise in-season workshops as part of the training calendar
  • Niseko receives some of the world's highest annual snowfall — accumulations are measured in metres across the season
  • Heavy snowfall periods bring low visibility days that affect both bookings and daily movement
  • Cold temperatures December through February; March begins to moderate

Tax

Because you are an independent contractor, the Japanese tax rules that apply to you are different from those for employees. Withholding rates are set by Japanese tax law. Snowsports instructors fall under the Sport Instructor category in the Japanese withholding tax framework, and FES withholds tax from each monthly invoice at the rate set by that framework.

Even on a working visa, you can be considered a tax resident of Japan if you can demonstrate that Japan is your living base. The final determination sits with the Japanese tax office. For tax residents, the amount refunded depends on your overall income and deductions. We strongly recommend researching this before the season begins — the difference between resident and non-resident treatment can be significant over the course of a season.

Interview Prep

Your Interview

Our goal in this interview is not simply to assess whether you're the right fit for FES — it's equally to help you assess whether FES is the right fit for you. We believe long-term careers only take root when a school and an instructor are genuinely aligned from both sides. For that reason, we ask as much as we share. Even if it turns out that FES and you aren't the best match for one another, we want to help you find the right place — we've spent years building relationships and knowledge across the snowsports industry, and we'd rather point you in the right direction than place you somewhere that doesn't serve your career.

The interview is a conversation, not a checklist. We explore four areas with every candidate — not to find someone who scores perfectly across all of them, but to understand how you think, how you operate, and where you want to go. Technical skiing or riding ability matters, but it is not the primary focus.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Pay

How does the 35% / 55% work exactly?
35% is what you receive when FES assigns you a client — they book through us, we allocate you the lesson. 55% is what you receive when you process your own booking end-to-end: you communicate with the client, create the invoice in our system, collect payment, and deliver the lesson. Both rates are approximate — actual figures vary within ±5% depending on credit card fees and agent commissions.
Can I increase my rate during the season?
Your blended rate increases as the proportion of OBP work rises. If half your days are assigned and half are OBP, your effective average is around 45%. The more clients you generate yourself, the higher your season-average rate.
Do I need to do consecutive seasons?
No. There is no back-to-back requirement. You can take a season off and return to FES. Your loyalty tier is based on total seasons with FES, not consecutive ones.

Accommodation

What does accommodation actually cost per month?
Roughly ¥85,000/month per room in a shared house (typically 3–4 people), plus utilities (~¥20,000–30,000/month) and a share of snow clearing costs (~¥200,000 split across the season). If you source your own accommodation, FES subsidises 1/3 of your rent up to ¥20,000/month.
When do I need to confirm my accommodation situation?
By August. This allows FES to plan housing allocation for the season.
What if my partner is coming but not working for FES?
FES members get priority for staff housing. If your partner is not working for FES, they are lower priority for a staff housing spot. This needs to be discussed individually.

Insurance

As an independent contractor, do I need my own insurance?
For the core risks — third-party liability, guest injury, and your personal injury treatment — no. FES covers all of these. You will end up paying less than 10% of any medical bills once Japanese health insurance and the FES private insurance layer are both applied. Freelance worker's compensation is optional at ~¥3,000/month, with FES covering half. What you do need your own cover for: lost income if you can't work, and travel insurance for personal belongings and flights. We strongly recommend travel insurance.

Visa

What types of visa do members come on?
Roughly half of our team are Japan residents. Of the other half, most are on a ski instructor visa sponsored by FES. Some are on a skilled labour visa, which requires 36+ months of documented, provable professional experience in the field.
Can FES sponsor my visa?
Yes, for eligible candidates. See the visa requirements document ↗ for qualifying categories and requirements. Send all documents to Yuka by 15 July.
Do I need a driver's licence?
Yes. You must hold a valid international driving permit recognised in Japan. Japan follows the Geneva Convention (1949) — not the Vienna Convention (1968). Make sure your permit is issued under the Geneva Convention before assuming it is valid here.

Japanese Language

Are there Japanese language classes in Niseko?
Yes. There is a language school called Smile in the area with qualified teachers. FES can also provide a Japanese language workshop upon request — speak to Yuka or raise it at your interview.

General

What's the difference between FES and a conventional ski school?
Conventional ski schools serve an essential role in the industry and we have real respect for what they've built. FES approaches things differently — instructors here operate as independent professionals within a shared platform, with genuine ownership over their client relationships, invoicing, and career development. The tradeoff is more responsibility. The return is better rates, real support, and a team that takes the profession seriously.
What does a typical season look like beyond teaching?
Beyond lessons, you're encouraged to participate in team training, workshops, and events. These aren't obligations — they earn points in the Rewards Programme and are how the team grows collectively. Workshops cover accounting, service, guiding, and mental health. Team events include overnight trips, night riding, and the annual photo shoot.
What does FES expect from me operationally?
Prompt communication (same day, every time), accurate reporting, calendar discipline, no assumptions, and guest-first thinking in every interaction. The operational standard is high — this is what we evaluate, and it's also what separates good instructors from great ones.
